# The story
I want the player to be a new guest visiting this hotel for the first time. Their goal is simple: reach their room, the honeymoon suite. The journey reveals that the hotel is a loop and a trap.

## Arrival
At night, my car stops on a California desert highway. A front tyre is flat and steam rises from the bonnet. I step out. The palm trees are silhouettes against the night sky. Red windows mark the hotel. Its sign reads HOTEL, followed by a word scratched beyond recognition. I walk to the entrance and go inside.

At reception, the woman with the brown bob looks up from the desk. A lift notice points me to the east stair. I head upstairs to my room. No one speaks.

## The way upstairs
I meet the staff on an ordinary first trip. In the Night Bar I can ask for wine. The bartender gestures to the vintage and his reply appears on screen. In the guest washroom I see my own face. On a later visit, that same face can be covered in blood. Later passages reveal anomalies. The guest continues when things are normal and turns back when something changes.

The stairs and halls make the route feel longer and stranger. Four correct decisions bring the guest to the suite. The short luggage puzzle interrupts the ascent without changing their goal.

The story includes the porter’s line about carrying a case upstairs yesterday. It does not establish that the player has visited before. Any dialogue I keep must support the first-time-guest setup.

## The trap
I open the honeymoon suite and find the woman who checked me in behind the desk again. Her face stays still as she slips between flesh and bone. Beside her, the bald butler rings his handbell.

The cast walks toward me. The child dribbles a head. Blood runs from the dog's mouth. The bartender pours wine down his jacket, and the attendant lifts a silver cover to show me another face. The butler grins and rings as his bones show through. The receptionist alone keeps a straight face. She fills the last shot. Blackout.

## My inspiration
I chose Hotel California as inspiration for the ending’s sense of being trapped in a hotel. Twin Peaks shapes the hostile reveal; Hokum shapes the hotel atmosphere; Exit 8 shapes the observation mechanic.

I also chose the wine-and-1969 exchange as a written reference in the bar.

## The final walk

After I clear four floors, walking toward the suite starts the last verse of the recording I supplied. The final steps become a fixed shot. As the singer remembers looking for a way back, the door returns me to reception. The camera turns to the approaching cast, follows each gesture and cuts back to the receptionist for the last warning. I can read the lyrics even with sound muted.

After the blackout, I let the guitar solo carry the game title and credits. My name comes first, then the music, disclaimer, inspirations and sources. I keep the title and credits to 84 seconds. The music fades over the final ten seconds as I thank David Lynch, wish him rest in peace, and close with a four-second thank-you to Professor Nathan Madrid.
