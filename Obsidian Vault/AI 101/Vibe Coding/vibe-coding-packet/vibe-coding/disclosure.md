# How I used AI
## What I directed
I chose Hotel of Horror, the hotel setting, the honeymoon-suite goal, the observation mechanic, the visual references and the hostile ending. I directed the cast, route, stairs, luggage puzzle, porter dialogue and the revisions after watching the tests.

I wanted one complete, polished storyline. I judged the output and asked for changes when the hands looked fake, the hotel felt crowded, or the rules needed explaining.

## What I used Codex for
I used Codex to develop the game and turn my direction into code, scenes, animation, documentation and deployments. It wrote the gameplay and interface code, ran Blender scripts, adapted models, generated procedural assets and performed developer checks. The earlier moodboards used image generation.

The 3D work used headless Blender and Python. I requested Blender MCP, but the records do not show confirmed MCP calls.

## Work from other creators
I used MakeHuman CC0 assets for the humans and 3dskillup.art’s CC BY 4.0 German Shepherd for the dog. I supplied the Angelo Badalamenti hotel recording and the Eagles ending recording. I used music by Kevin MacLeod in earlier builds. Three.js and the fonts are external dependencies. [Full credits, licences and adaptations](sources/assets-and-tools.md).

## Testing
I watched the live tests and supplied feedback while Codex operated the controls. Developer checks are recorded separately. Cycle 1 is complete. I supplied the [Cycle 2 Test 1 findings](build-record/tests/formal-cycle-2/T1-result.md) from my own playtest. I directed the [Test 2 movement and ending revisions](build-record/tests/formal-cycle-2/T2-result.md). Tests 3–4 are pending.
