# How I used AI during the build
I used Codex to implement and revise Room 0 from my direction. This is a short index of the consequential work. The [original detailed log](../sources/archive/documentation-before-voice-edit-2026-11-11/build-record/ai-use-log.md) keeps the fuller prompt and tool record.

| Stage | My direction | What Codex produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept | I chose Room 0 after exploring the train, rover, Poe and other ideas. | Drafts of the plan, story and mechanics |
| Visual direction | I chose Visual Study 02 and wanted a hybrid build close to that moodboard. | Blender scenes, rendered hotel backgrounds and a Three.js prototype |
| Characters | I rejected blocky figures and asked for realistic, rigged characters. | Adapted MakeHuman cast, authored animation and model exports |
| Loop | I wanted Exit 8-style randomized anomalies and four correct decisions to the suite. | Passage rules, ordinary/changed encounters, scoring and resets |
| Ending | I chose the return to reception, glowing skeleton and surrounding cast. | Scripted reveal, sound cues, glow and replay |
| Atmosphere | I asked for better lighting, movement, UI and free music with the right mood. | Art and animation passes, clearer interface and licensed music integration |
| Defense | I questioned the holy item and chose to remove it. | Retreat-only threat encounters |
| Test 1 | I called out unnatural hands, the fake dog and weak text. | v14 hand repair, credited replacement dog and heavier lettering |
| Tests 2–3 | I wanted clearer guidance, separate encounters and more floors and horror. | v15 register, stair/gallery routes and closer anomaly shots |
| Luggage room | I chose one porter/mirror puzzle before Test 4. | v16 claim-ticket interaction |
| Test 4 | I wanted a short character scene and specified the welcome and broken lift. | v17 reception sequence and luggage recognition |
| Visual polish | I asked to improve everything on screen. | v18 renders and material/reflection improvements |
| Documentation | I asked for clear model credits, accurate Blender attribution and first-person writing. | Source audit, credits and shorter project notes |

The 3D workflow used headless Blender scripts. MCP execution is not confirmed. [Asset and tool credits](../sources/assets-and-tools.md).

I supplied observations while Codex controlled and recorded the tests. [Actual results](testing-and-failure-log.md). I played again and gave [Cycle 2 Test 1 feedback](tests/formal-cycle-2/T1-result.md). Codex verified and recorded the later edits.

**November 11 story decision:** I clarified that the player is a new guest on their first visit. The broken lift sends them upstairs to the honeymoon suite, where returning to reception reveals the loop and trap. I named Hotel California as the ending inspiration. Codex updated the story, PRD, idea, ending decision and source note. This was a documentation update.

**November 11 title decision:** I named the game Hotel California and asked for anomalies based on the song. Codex renamed the game files and main notes and drafted three ordinary/changed encounter pairs for review. The pool is unchanged.

**Anomaly direction:** I asked for ideas based on the song’s more recognizable lyrics and proposed a Twin Peaks-inspired bathroom mirror showing a bloodied version of myself. Codex replaced the ceiling-mirror proposal and drafted further checkout, feast, night-attendant and wine concepts for my review. No new encounters were built.

I chose Hotel of Horror as the title and directed a California desert breakdown before check-in. I wanted a flat tyre, smoke from the bonnet, palms, red hotel windows and a female receptionist. Codex staged the opening, fitted her bob and formal outfit in Blender, and kept the older butler in the gallery and ending.

I asked for a higher-quality rigged vehicle, a full-night exterior and a sign with only HOTEL readable. Codex sourced Car Concept, authored the driver’s exit and door contacts, revised the lighting, and documented the creators and licence.

I provided a reference for the receptionist’s face and hairstyle. Codex fitted the rigged face, rebuilt the side-parted waves, rendered a portrait study and checked the exported model in the reception scene.
