# The anomalies
I wanted the hotel’s people to feel as unsettling as the place itself. I spread them across the stairs and gallery, keeping the whole gathering for the ending.

| Encounter | Ordinary | Changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Butler | Holds his bell | Bell floats; he approaches |
| Room service | Closed cloche | Something knocks underneath |
| Children | Roll a red ball | Roll a porcelain likeness of a third child |
| Dog | Standing dog, standing shadow | Standing dog, seated shadow |
| Last order | Server sets the glass down, steps her hand back and lets the bartender pick it up | Released glass rises into a waiting hand |
| Traveller | Moves with his reflection | Reflection stays behind, then turns |
| Founder portrait | Looks away | Faces the player; a shadow escapes the frame |
| Rain | Falls down | Middle window rains upward |
| Lift | Doors closed | Gap reveals the hotel wallpaper inside |
| Guest washroom | My face and clothes look clean in the mirror | Blood covers my reflected cheek, collar and lapel. I still look clean outside the glass |
| Coat | Hangs empty | Stretches sideways from the hanger |
| Runner | Lies flat | Rises and falls as if breathing |

I gave the ordinary staff short written greetings and small smiles. In the Night Bar, I can ask for wine once. The bartender gestures toward a bottle and answers with the 1969 line. The moving glass is the separate clue I judge on scored visits.

The game randomizes the order. The player continues if nothing changed or turns back if something did. The butler is the active threat.
